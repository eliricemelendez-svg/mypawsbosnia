Rescue Dogs Available for Adoption in Europe

12 rescue dogs at our shelter in Bosnia are ready to travel to families across Europe, the UK, and Switzerland. Every dog is fully vetted, vaccinated, and issued an pet passport.

The rescue dog situation in Bosnia

Bosnia has tens of thousands of stray dogs. There is no national spay/neuter programme, and public shelters are severely underfunded. Dogs in municipal shelters face overcrowding, disease, and in many cases euthanasia.

My Paws Bosnia (Moje Sape) is a private rescue shelter in Brcko, run by Vesna and her family since 2019. We rescue dogs from the streets and from public shelters, provide full veterinary care, and find them permanent homes across Europe. We have placed 5 dogs into families so far, and 12 more are currently waiting.

Why these dogs make great pets

Socialised and people-friendly

Our dogs live together at the shelter with daily human contact. They are used to people, other dogs, and in many cases children and cats. They are not feral animals.

Fully vetted before travel

Every dog is neutered/spayed, vaccinated (including rabies), dewormed, treated for parasites, microchipped, and issued an pet passport. Blood tests for brucellosis are available on request.

Temperament-assessed

Vesna knows every dog individually. She can tell you exactly how they behave with children, cats, other dogs, and strangers. We match dogs to families based on real daily observation, not guesswork.

Mixed breeds with fewer health problems

Our dogs are mixed breeds. Common mixes include Labrador, Shepherd, Spaniel-Setter, Retriever-Spitz, and Terrier crosses. Mixed breeds tend to have fewer inherited health issues and longer lifespans than purebreds.

How adoption works

1

Message us

Send us a message on Facebook or WhatsApp. Tell us which dog you are interested in and about your home.

2

Home check

We do a quick, friendly home check. In the UK this is usually a video call. In Germany and Austria, it will most likely be in person.

3

Vet work and passport

We complete all vaccinations, neutering, deworming, and issue the pet passport.

4

Transport to you

A licensed transporter brings your dog directly to your home. The whole process takes 4 to 8 weeks.

For the full step-by-step guide, read our adoption guide.

Frequently asked questions

Where can these rescue dogs be adopted to? +
Our dogs can travel to any EU country, the UK, and Switzerland. We have placed dogs in England, Belgium, Austria, Germany, the Netherlands, and France.
Are these dogs safe around children? +
Most of our dogs are good with children. Each dog's profile includes specific information about their temperament around kids. We always match dogs to families based on the home environment.
What breeds are available? +
Our dogs are mixed breeds. Common mixes include Labrador, Shepherd, Spaniel-Setter, Retriever-Spitz, and Terrier crosses. Mixed breed dogs tend to have fewer inherited health problems and longer lifespans than purebreds.
How do I know these dogs are healthy? +
Every dog is examined by a veterinarian, vaccinated (including rabies), neutered or spayed, dewormed, treated for parasites, microchipped, and issued an pet passport before travel. Blood tests for brucellosis and other conditions are available on request.
